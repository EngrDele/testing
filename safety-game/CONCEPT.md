# SAFE SITE: A Board Game That Replaces the Induction Quiz

*Research and concept note for a safety-induction game modelled on the Lagos edition of Monopoly*

---

## 1. Background: what the "Lagos game" is

The "Lagos game" is **Monopoly: City of Lagos Edition**.

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Original game | Monopoly, owned by **Hasbro** (USA). Every country and city edition uses the same rules. |
| Lagos adaptation | Launched in **December 2012** at Lagos City Hall. It was the first African city to get its own Monopoly edition. |
| Who made it | **Bestman Games** (founder Nimi Akinkugbe). Hasbro gave them the rights to make local editions for Africa, except South Africa. |
| Supporters | Lagos State Government, First Bank and GTBank. |
| What changed | Only the **skin** changed. The rules stayed the same. Banana Island took Mayfair's place as the most expensive property and Makoko became the cheapest. Squares included the Nigerian Stock Exchange, Murtala Muhammed Airport and well-known hotels. The cards were rewritten for Nigerian situations. |
| Stated purpose | Pride in the city, its history and landmarks, **and teaching people about laws that are often ignored**. |
| Later editions | Electronic Banking edition (bank cards instead of paper money), Nigeria Centenary, Cross River State, Accra, and a Corona School edition. There was also a schools tournament; King's College won in 2013. |

**What we copy from it:**
1. **Keep a mechanic people already know and change the content to local content.** Players don't spend time learning rules. They spend it on the content.
2. **People love seeing their own places on the board.** Lagosians liked finding Banana Island and Makoko. Workers will like finding *their own* gate, generator house and warehouse.
3. **A game can teach rules.** Bestman Games and Lagos State already said this was one of the game's goals.
4. **One template can be reprinted for many sites.** Bestman made many editions from one base game. We can make one edition per company or per site.

> **Legal note:** Do not use the Monopoly name, logo, board artwork or card designs. Hasbro owns them, and Bestman only uses them under licence. Rules ideas such as "move around a board, land on squares, draw cards" are free to use. SAFE SITE needs its own name and artwork.

## 2. The problem with the induction we have now

The usual induction is: **watch a video, then tick a 10-question paper quiz.**

| Weakness | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Watching is passive | People drift off, check their phones, or sleep in an air-conditioned room. |
| The quiz tests memory, not judgement | "What colour is the fire extinguisher for electrical fires?" is not the same as *noticing* a blocked extinguisher on a busy day. |
| Answers get shared | The same paper has been used for years and the answers go around the canteen. |
| The video is generic | It is often foreign stock footage. It does not show *our* compound, *our* generator house or *our* forklift route. |
| It happens once | Nothing reinforces it after day 1, so people forget most of it within weeks. |
| The tick only proves attendance | It does not show the person is competent. This is weak evidence after an accident or in an audit. |
| Language and literacy | Some workers, cleaners, drivers and casuals struggle with English on paper. |

Research supports a game-based approach, although the evidence is still thin. In a controlled study, construction-safety training delivered as a serious game raised safety knowledge more than traditional training, and the gain lasted over time ([Kazar & Comu, 2021](https://openaccess.gedik.edu.tr/items/22d1eb0a-e1f3-4282-a8e0-b8aa460d800d)). In a separate study on emergency evacuation, people kept knowledge from a game longer than knowledge from printed material ([Univ. of Udine](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/ee33d9c2-9cdc-488b-81ac-29cd42a94ec8/Improving_Knowledge_Retention_and_Perceived_Control_through_Serious_Games_a_Study_about_Assisted_Emergency_Evacuation.pdf)). Both studies are small, so our own pilot should measure results (see §7).

## 3. The proposal in one line

> **Keep the video, but make it short. Replace the paper quiz with three things: a board game of *your own compound*, a real hazard hunt on site, and short practical drills. After that, keep the learning fresh on WhatsApp.**

This builds on your idea of replaying real-life scenarios of the compound instead of using a paper quiz. Here is the full structure:

```
 BEFORE DAY 1        DAY 1 (≈ 2.5–3 hrs)                                  AFTER
┌───────────┐   ┌──────────┐  ┌────────────┐  ┌───────────┐  ┌────────┐  ┌──────────────────┐
│ 8-min     │ → │ SAFE SITE│→ │ LIVE HAZARD│→ │ PRACTICAL │→ │ BADGE  │→ │ WhatsApp scenario│
│ phone     │   │ board    │  │ HUNT on    │  │ DRILLS    │  │ issued │  │ every week, near-│
│ video     │   │ game     │  │ real site  │  │ (fire,    │  │        │  │ miss rewards,    │
│ (own site)│   │ (60 min) │  │ (30 min)   │  │ muster..) │  │        │  │ monthly league   │
└───────────┘   └──────────┘  └────────────┘  └───────────┘  └────────┘  └──────────────────┘
```

## 4. The SAFE SITE board game

### 4.1 Same idea as the Lagos edition: a familiar game in a local setting

| Monopoly / Lagos edition | SAFE SITE |
|---|---|
| City streets and landmarks | **Zones of the company's own compound**, with photos of the real places |
| Banana Island (most valuable) | **Highest-risk zone**, e.g. Diesel Tank Farm, High-Voltage Room or Confined Space |
| Makoko (cheapest) | **Lowest-risk zone**, e.g. Reception or Car Park. Low risk still means some risk. |
| GO: collect ₦200 | **TOOLBOX TALK**: collect Safety Points and one new rule card |
| Chance cards | **HAZARD cards**: a scene happens and you must decide what to do |
| Community Chest cards | **NEAR-MISS cards**: real stories from this site or the industry ("Last year at our warehouse…") |
| Jail | **STOP-WORK / INVESTIGATION**: you miss turns, and to get out you explain what went wrong and how to prevent it |
| Go to Jail | **"Oga said just do it"**: you followed an unsafe instruction, so go to Investigation |
| Free Parking | **ASSEMBLY POINT**: everyone checks in and the group earns a bonus |
| Income Tax | **"Shortcut tax"**: you lose points for a shortcut, such as skipping the permit or the PPE |
| Railway stations (4) | **The 4 emergency points**: Fire Alarm, First-Aid Box, Emergency Phone, Muster Point |
| Utilities (2) | **Generator House** and **Power Changeover**. These are Lagos realities: CO fumes, back-feeding, diesel |
| Houses and hotels | **Controls you add to a zone, following the hierarchy of controls.** Signage and PPE count as 1 house. A guard or barrier counts as 2. Engineering controls count as 3. **Eliminate or substitute** counts as a hotel. |
| Rent | Not used for money. When a player lands on a zone, the **zone owner reads a scenario card** to them. A wrong answer costs points. A right answer earns points for both players. |
| Money | **Safety Points** (paper or app) |

### 4.2 Make it co-operative, with some competition

Pure Monopoly is about bankrupting other players. That is the wrong message for safety, where the goal is **"everyone goes home safe."** SAFE SITE therefore adds a **Site Safety Meter** that all players share:

- The meter starts at 10.
- A wrong answer on a red-level hazard card drops the meter.
- Unsafe shortcuts drop it.
- Spotting hazards and reporting near-misses raises it.
- **If the meter reaches 0, there is a "Major Incident" and *every player loses*, including the leader.**
- If the group survives the set number of rounds, **everyone passes**, and the top scorer wins a small prize such as airtime or canteen credit.

This teaches the main lesson: *your unsafe act can hurt me too, so I should speak up.*

### 4.3 Example board (a mid-size Lagos manufacturing or logistics compound)

The board is printed from each company's own site map. A 40-square example:

```
 0 TOOLBOX TALK (GO)      10 STOP-WORK/INVESTIGATION    20 ASSEMBLY POINT          30 "OGA SAID JUST DO IT"
 1 Main Gate & Security   11 Workshop / Grinding        21 Loading Bay             31 Chemical Store
 2 NEAR-MISS card         12 GENERATOR HOUSE (utility)  22 HAZARD card             32 Laboratory
 3 Visitor Car Park       13 Electrical Panel Room      23 Forklift Route          33 NEAR-MISS card
 4 SHORTCUT TAX           14 Compressor Room            24 Racking Aisle           34 Effluent Plant
 5 FIRE ALARM (emergency) 15 FIRST-AID BOX (emergency) 25 EMERGENCY PHONE         35 MUSTER POINT (emergency)
 6 Reception              16 Canteen / Kitchen (gas)    26 Production Line 1       36 HAZARD card
 7 HAZARD card            17 NEAR-MISS card             27 Production Line 2       37 Roof / Work at Height
 8 Admin Offices          18 Staff Clinic               28 POWER CHANGEOVER (util) 38 SHORTCUT TAX
 9 Staircase & Corridors  19 Waste/Scrap Yard           29 Boiler House            39 DIESEL TANK FARM (top)
```

### 4.4 Example cards (with local language)

Each card shows a **real photo from the site**. It has 2–3 choices and a short "why". Cards can be printed in **English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo**.

**HAZARD card: Generator House**
> NEPA has taken light. The generator is running and the door is closed because of the rain. Your colleague is lying down inside to rest.
> **A)** Leave him, the rain is heavy. **B)** Open the door, wake him, bring him out to fresh air and report it. **C)** Off the gen.
> ✅ **B first.** Generator fumes contain carbon monoxide, which you cannot smell, and it kills people who are sleeping. Get the person out to fresh air, ventilate the room, then report so the generator can be shut down safely and the exhaust checked. If you choose C alone, the person is still inside breathing the fumes. *Na quiet killer be gen smoke.*

**HAZARD card: Forklift Route**
> You are carrying a carton and you want to cut through the forklift lane to reach the canteen quickly.
> ✅ Use the marked pedestrian walkway. The forklift driver cannot see you behind the load. +2 points.

**NEAR-MISS card: Loading Bay (true story format)**
> "A trailer driver drove off while a loader was still inside the truck. The loader jumped and sprained his ankle." What control would stop this happening again?
> ✅ Wheel chocks, keys handed to the bay supervisor, and a traffic light or sign at the bay. *Discuss as a group. Everyone earns +1 to the Safety Meter for a good answer.*

**HAZARD card: Main Gate**
> An okada brings you to the gate. Your hands are full and you have no ID card. Security says "Go in, I know you."
> ✅ Wait and get a visitor pass. Security exists to control who is on site in an emergency, because the muster count depends on it.

**"OGA SAID JUST DO IT" (Go to Investigation)**
> Supervisor: "Abeg, climb that rack and bring the box down. No time for ladder."
> To leave Investigation: state your **Stop-Work Authority** and name the safe alternative.

**HAZARD card: Rainy season**
> The compound is flooded near the electrical panel room. Water is entering.
> ✅ Keep away, warn others, and call the electrician or emergency number. Do not walk through water near live equipment.

**SHORTCUT TAX**
> You did not wear your safety boots because "it is just for 5 minutes". Pay 3 points. *Accident no dey give notice.*

### 4.5 Card levels and the facilitator

- **Green**: general awareness, for everyone, including visitors and cleaners.
- **Amber**: area-specific, such as warehouse or production.
- **Red**: life-saving rules, such as work at height, permit-to-work, energy isolation (LOTO), confined space, hot work and lifting. A wrong red answer **always** drops the Safety Meter, and the facilitator explains why.
- An **HSE facilitator** acts as the "bank" and runs the discussion. The game works best with **4–6 players** per board for about **60 minutes**.
- There is a **scorecard per player**. The facilitator notes every red card a person got wrong, and that person reviews those topics again before getting the badge.

### 4.6 Digital twin (phase 2)

The same game can run as an **app or web game**:
- Players move through **photos or 360° images of the real compound**.
- Answers are recorded automatically, which gives an audit trail.
- The cards are mixed differently every time, so there is no answer sheet to share.
- It can be played on a phone at the gate by visitors and drivers, as a 5-minute "Gate Edition".
- It can be played offline, which helps where network is poor.

## 5. The other replacements

### 5.1 Short, local video *before* day 1
- **6–8 minutes, filmed in the real compound with real staff.** Do not use foreign stock footage.
- It is sent on WhatsApp the day before, with Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo subtitles or voice-over.
- It ends with the message: *"Tomorrow you go play SAFE SITE. Look well, everything you see here go dey the game."*

### 5.2 Live hazard hunt ("Spot it, Tag it")
- HSE sets up **8–12 deliberate hazards** in a safe, controlled area of the real compound. Examples: an extinguisher with a broken seal, a blocked fire exit, a frayed cable, a missing machine guard, an unlabelled chemical bottle, a ladder at the wrong angle and a missing wheel chock.
- In pairs, new staff get **15 minutes** to find them and **red-tag** them, with a photo on a phone.
- The **pass mark is to find all life-critical hazards.** Missing a red one means going round again with the facilitator.
- This is your "replay of real-life scenarios of the compound", done physically as well as on the board.

### 5.3 Practical drills (5–10 min each)
- Use a fire extinguisher with the PASS technique, on a training fire pan or a water extinguisher.
- Walk the actual escape route to the muster point and be counted.
- Call the emergency number and give location, what happened and how many people are hurt, using a script card.
- Put on PPE correctly and inspect it.
- For site staff only: a demonstration of lock-out/tag-out on a dummy panel.

### 5.4 Proof of competence, not a tick
- When a person passes all three parts, they get a **badge level** sticker on their ID card. For example: **Green = Visitor or office**, **Amber = Area access**, **Red = Permit-holder**, given after more training.
- Records are kept digitally: game score, hazard-hunt photos and drill sign-off. This is much stronger evidence for an audit or after an incident than a ticked paper.

### 5.5 After day 1: keep it alive
- **Weekly WhatsApp scenario**: one photo from the site and one question that takes 30 seconds. Use spaced repetition at days 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 and 90.
- **Near-miss reporting rewards**: the best near-miss report each month becomes a **new card in the game**, with the reporter's permission and name. People like seeing their own story in the deck.
- **Monthly league** between departments, with a quarterly SAFE SITE tournament similar to the Lagos schools Monopoly tournament.
- **Refresher**: 20 minutes of SAFE SITE each year instead of re-watching the video.

## 6. Who gets which version

| Audience | Version | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors and delivery drivers | Gate Edition: phone, 10 cards, green only | 5–10 min |
| Contractors | Board or app with contractor deck, hazard hunt and permit-to-work cards | 90 min |
| New office staff | Full board game with green and amber cards, hazard hunt and fire drill | ~2.5 hrs |
| New operations or site staff | Full programme, all levels, LOTO and work-at-height drills | ~3 hrs + on-the-job |
| Supervisors and managers | "Oga" Edition: they hold the zones, decide on stop-work, and play the investigator role | 90 min |

## 7. How we know it works (pilot plan)

1. **Pick one site.** Run the old induction (video and quiz) with one intake and SAFE SITE with the next.
2. **Measure both groups the same way:**
   - Scenario test on day 1 and again at **day 30 and day 90**, using photos, not the old quiz.
   - Hazard-spotting score on a fresh hazard-hunt layout at day 30.
   - Near-miss reports raised per person in the first 90 days. This is a leading indicator: more reports are good.
   - First-90-days incidents and first-aid cases (lagging; small numbers).
   - Feedback: "Did you enjoy it? Would you speak up to your supervisor?"
3. **Decide after 3 months** whether to roll out to other sites.

**Fits with standards:** The programme supports **ISO 45001** clauses on *competence* (7.2), *awareness* (7.3) and *worker participation* (5.4). It also fits the Lagos State Safety Commission's push for documented safe systems of work, including its "Working Safely" and "Safety First" programmes. Confirm the exact legal training duties with your HSE or legal adviser.

## 8. What is needed to build it

| Item | Effort |
|---|---|
| Site photos and a walk-through with HSE to list the top 40 hazards | 1–2 days |
| Write about 120 cards (60 hazard, 30 near-miss, 30 rules), then translate them | 1–2 weeks |
| Graphic design of the board, cards and tokens (own brand, **not** Monopoly artwork) | 1–2 weeks |
| Print 5–10 board sets (Lagos printers; heavy board and laminated cards) | 1 week |
| Hazard-hunt kit (tags, staged items) and facilitator guide | 3–5 days |
| Optional: phone or web version of the game | 4–8 weeks |

**Business angle:** Every company's compound is different, so each one needs its own edition. This is the Bestman model: one base game and many editions. It could be sold as a service: *we photograph your site, write your cards, print your board, train your facilitators, and run the WhatsApp follow-up.*

---

## 9. Summary

- The Lagos game showed that **a familiar game with local, recognisable content gets attention and can teach rules.**
- SAFE SITE uses the same approach for safety induction. It uses the **company's own compound**, **real scenarios**, **local language**, and a **shared Safety Meter** so that everybody loses if one person cuts corners.
- The paper quiz is replaced by **a game, a live hazard hunt and practical drills.** These show what a person *can do*, not only what they remember.
- Learning continues after day 1 through **WhatsApp scenarios, near-miss stories that become new cards, and leagues.**
